“Orange” reflects on the R-Gan Junior Life Co-creation Camp

Before coming to Tieniu Village, I always imagined that a rural co-creation camp would be boring and hard to get through. Staying home with my phone seemed a much more comfortable choice than going to the countryside.

My dad had known Tieniu Village for a long time. Between 2020 and 2026, he visited eight or nine times and often shared stories about the village with me. When he heard about the youth co-creation camp, he immediately suggested that I join.

At first, I was very nervous. Most of the other participants were in middle or high school, and I worried that I would struggle to fit in. Thinking I could just give it a try and have some fun, I agreed to go.

On July 27, Dad and I set out from Shenzhen and traveled to Tieniu Village with the others in our group.

In fact, this was not my first visit. In January this year, the four of us in my family had joined an R-Gan harvest festival and adopted a fruit tree. So when the car stopped in the village again, that familiar feeling returned.

The first stop on this R-Gan Junior camp was Nanbao Mountain. Soon after arriving, I played basketball with some of the older participants. The awkwardness of being strangers quickly disappeared, and I soon felt part of this warm group.

In Tieniu Village, sharing tea had a special sense of occasion. We sat together to taste it, set aside the worries of school and daily life—and, for a while, the pressures of work—and closed our eyes to take in the fragrance. We could simply enjoy that relaxed, happy moment.

Sharing tea with my companions

What I remember most was cooking together in the fields. We split into groups: one tended the fire and cooked noodles, another prepared ingredients and cooked the dishes, and another decorated and set the table. Tasks were not assigned by gender; everyone contributed what they could.

I was in the fire group, fanning the flames and adding firewood. As the smoke slowly rose, I stayed beside the fire and felt the warmth of cooking outdoors.

Uncle “Big Corn” and me lighting and fanning the fire

When lunch was served at 12:10, I took a bite of the tomato and scrambled eggs I had helped prepare. I tasted more than delicious food: I felt the deep satisfaction of everyone’s work and effort. In that moment, I truly understood how worthwhile it had all been.

Eating the lunch we had helped make with “Little Tree” and “Honey”

When I opened the window, fresh country air came rushing in. Putting down my phone and walking along the wooded paths, I discovered a vibrant, lively side of village life.

This was my first time away from my family for so long, and I had worried that I might feel lonely and lost. But the warmth and kindness of everyone at camp surrounded me. I did not feel the loneliness I had imagined at all.

There was none of the city’s noisy traffic here. All around me were the clear sounds of nature.

Through its hills, everyday cooking, and human warmth, Tieniu Village showed me that when we step away from our screens and toward the land and the people around us, we can find a simple but deeply fulfilling kind of happiness.
