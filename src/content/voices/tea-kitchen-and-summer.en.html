Hi everyone! I'm Ruoyin, though you can also call me Ruby.

I'm an introvert, but once you get to know me, I become very outgoing. Please feel free to start a conversation—I promise I won't turn you away!

I especially love dancing. Ballet is my favorite, and I have been studying it for ten years.

Beyond dance, I enjoy baking, cooking, crochet, and making things by hand. I also love trying anything new.

If we share an interest, I would love to talk about it—and perhaps we could even make something interesting together.

I moved from Shanghai to Los Angeles four years ago, and I am now a ninth-grade student.

When I first arrived in the United States, almost everything felt unfamiliar. I had to adapt slowly, from the language to the culture.

Yet those experiences also helped me meet new friends, step beyond my comfort zone, and grow in ways I had not expected.

My Story with Tea

My maternal grandfather, whom I call Agong, was the person who always led me into the worlds of Zen tea and the tea mountain.

Ever since I was young, he often travelled there to attend Zen tea courses and volunteer.

When he came home, he would prepare tea and gather our family for a tea session. As we drank together, he shared the spirit of Zen tea that he had learned on the mountain.

Little by little, I became interested in tea. I came to see it as a way of living that helps a person grow quiet and enter into conversation with themselves.

Two years ago, Agong took me to the tea mountain for the first time.

I had drunk tea with him since childhood, but only after arriving there did I begin to understand the meaning of Zen tea more deeply.

Warming the bowl, adding the leaves, pouring the water, and watching each leaf slowly open—every step asks for attention, patience, and care.

I love the atmosphere of the tea mountain.

People care for and help one another, creating a place that feels warm and at ease.

Whatever their age or background, everyone is willing to share what they know and learn together.

I realized that health does not come from the body alone. It also grows through sincere relationships and a positive, peaceful environment.

Whenever I visit the tea mountain, I leave feeling lighter. I hope to carry the attitude toward life that I learn there back into my everyday world.

Last year, Agong passed away, but his influence has never left me.

Whenever I prepare a pot of tea, I remember drinking with him as a child and listening to his stories of the mountain.

The bond with tea that he gave me did not end when he left. Instead, it made me treasure even more every moment I spend with tea and with my family.

I hope to keep learning Zen tea in the years ahead, carrying forward the warmth and way of living that Agong passed on to me.

My Story with Food

The kitchen on the tea mountain has stayed with me just as vividly.

Its food is made from natural, fresh ingredients and centers on light vegetarian dishes. Instead of complicated seasoning, the cooks try to preserve each ingredient's original flavor.

That kitchen changed my assumptions about vegetarian food. Every dish allowed me to taste the ingredients as they truly were.

There, I came to understand healthy eating as a way of caring for the body.

Even a simple meal can leave you deeply satisfied, and it made me pay closer attention to my own eating habits.

This way of eating showed me that simple, wholesome food can bring happiness. It taught me to value the health of both body and mind, and a way of life closer to nature.

Each time I entered the kitchen, I saw people working quietly.

They washed every ingredient carefully and prepared each dish with patience, filling the room with warmth and intention.

Everyone divided the work and moved together. I began to see that cooking is not merely a way to fill our stomachs; it can be a practice of serving a community and caring for others.

The kitchen was quiet and orderly, with an atmosphere of calm concentration.

When I joined the work, it felt different from cooking at home.

The tea mountain kitchen taught me how to choose healthy, natural food. It also taught me to approach every small part of life with gratitude, attention, and care.

I believe the attitude I learned in that kitchen will stay with me. It will shape not only how I eat, but how I live.
